# Mini-Retirement

You are helping the user plan a **mini-retirement** — Tim's term for a 1-6 month relocation abroad (or sometimes just to a different city) that distributes the "retirement" experience across one's working life rather than saving it for the end.

The book's argument: retiring at 65 is a bet that you'll still be physically and mentally able to enjoy what you wanted at 32. Mini-retirements take some of that risk off the table by spreading it.

## Before Starting

Read `lifestyle-design.md` and `dreamline.md`. Specifically:

- Is the mini-retirement on the dreamline? Where? When?
- What's the user's TDI? Income source?
- Family / partner / kids / pets — anyone or anything coming?

Then ask the **single most important question**:

> "What does the ideal day look like — wherever you go? Walk me through 6am to 10pm of a normal Tuesday."

The user's answer reveals more than any destination list. If they can describe the day in detail, the destination is almost always implied. If they can't describe the day, run `/dreamlining` first — they don't know what they want yet.

## Step 1: Pick the destination

Tim's heuristic: **the destination is downstream of the lifestyle.** Don't pick "Buenos Aires" and reverse-engineer the life. Pick the life and then the city that best fits.

Walk the user through the **destination filter**:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Climate | What's a non-starter? (Cold winters, monsoon, extreme heat, no seasons?) |
| Language | Comfortable with not speaking it, or want to learn? |
| Food | Coffee culture, food culture, dietary needs |
| Pace of life | Busy global city, mid-sized livable, small village |
| Community | Solo escape, expat hub, family-friendly |
| Time zone | Must overlap with US/EU work hours? |
| Cost of living | Working-class, middle-class, premium |
| Visa friendliness | US/EU passport friendly; what's the visa setup |
| Healthcare | Tourist-grade fine, or chronic-condition serious? |
| Travel cost in/out | One flight, two flights, weekly options |
| Safety | Solo female traveler / family / health considerations |
| Air quality | Often missed, often important |

Make a shortlist of **3 candidate cities**, with one being slightly outside the user's comfort zone.

Common 4HWW-spirit destinations (calibrate to current realities — costs and visa rules shift):

- Lisbon, Portugal
- Mexico City / Oaxaca / Mérida, Mexico
- Buenos Aires, Argentina
- Medellín, Colombia
- Chiang Mai, Thailand
- Bali (Canggu / Ubud), Indonesia
- Tbilisi, Georgia
- Madeira, Portugal
- Lecce / Bologna, Italy
- Cape Town, South Africa
- Kyoto, Japan

Don't take this list as a recommendation; use it as a sanity check that the user's shortlist isn't too narrow.

## Step 2: Length and timing

Three lengths work especially well:

- **30 days**: minimum useful — long enough to settle into a routine, short enough to not require complex setup
- **60-90 days**: the sweet spot — feels like a different life
- **6 months**: a full mini-retirement — produces real perspective shift; requires more setup

Ask the user which feels right. Push back gently if they pick 30 days because it feels safer — sometimes 60 is better even at the same total cost.

Then pin specific dates:

- **Departure date**
- **Return date** (if any)

Calendar them. Most mini-retirements that don't have specific dates never happen.

## Step 3: Budget

Build a quick budget for the destination. The 6 categories:

| Category | Range |
|---|---|
| Accommodation | $1,500-4,000/mo for a decent flat in most popular destinations |
| Food | $400-1,500/mo (cooking vs. eating out) |
| Local transport | $50-300/mo |
| Health insurance | $50-200/mo (travel insurance like SafetyWing, World Nomads, etc.) |
| Activities / fun | $200-1,000/mo |
| Buffer (10-20%) | because something always |

Total range for most destinations: **$3,000-8,000/mo** for a single person.

Compare to user's TDI × 30. If they're net-positive, great. If not, this is where automation matters — see the income side below.

## Step 4: The income side

Three patterns for mini-retirement income, each with its own sub-skill:

### Pattern A: Employed, going remote

User has a job and wants to work remotely from the destination.

- Run `/remote-work-proposal` if not already approved.
- Negotiate **time zone overlap** — usually 4-5 hours of overlap is enough for most knowledge work. (Lisbon + US east coast: 5 hours overlap.)
- Tax / legal considerations — know the 183-day rule.

### Pattern B: Muse / business owner

User has a muse generating income.

- Run `/income-autopilot` to make sure the muse runs without the user during absence.
- Pre-negotiate with key partners (suppliers, payment processors, support team) about the absence.
- Set up a single "founder check-in" cadence — once a week, 30 minutes — and stick to it.

### Pattern C: Saved runway

User is taking a true unpaid sabbatical.

- Calculate runway: cash on hand / monthly burn during the trip = months of safety.
- Recommend a minimum of **2 months of buffer beyond the trip end date** — re-entry isn't free.

## Step 5: What to wind down at home

The most-skipped step.

For each item, capture:

- Apartment / housing (sublet? short-term rent? leave empty?)
- Car (sell? store? hand to family?)
- Mail (forward? hold? digital?)
- Bills (autopay everything 60 days before departure)
- Pets (boarding? family? sitter?)
- Plants (give away — most don't survive 90+ days alone)
- Subscriptions (pause / cancel)
- Driver's license / address (do not change unless permanent)
- Phone plan (international roaming or local SIM)
- Banking (verify cards work abroad, raise daily limits, set travel notice)
- Healthcare (refill chronic prescriptions, dentist appt, eye exam)
- Voting / civic mail
- Family / partner / kids' school logistics

Print this as a checklist. Block one weekend 4 weeks before departure to clear it.

## Step 6: What to set up at destination

Pre-departure work:

- First 2 weeks of accommodation booked (don't commit to all 3 months upfront — you'll move once you're there)
- Local SIM or e-SIM ordered
- 2-3 candidate gyms / climbing / yoga / running clubs identified
- 2-3 candidate co-working spaces identified
- Visa research (if applicable) and any documents printed
- Currency / cash for first 48 hours
- One social anchor — a friend, an introduction, a meetup, a class — for the first week so you don't disappear into your room

After arrival, week 1 priorities:

1. Find the **morning ritual location** (coffee shop, park, beach)
2. Find **2 places that will become "yours"** (the gym, the cafe, the running route)
3. Make **one local friend or recurring acquaintance** (the yoga teacher, the barista, the language tutor)

These three things, more than any tourist itinerary, are what convert a vacation into a mini-retirement.

## Step 7: The work / non-work split

This is where most mini-retirements fail. Decide *before* leaving:

- How many hours a week will you actually work?
- During which times of day?
- What is explicitly off the table (e.g., "no work after 2pm local time")?

Without this, two things happen: either work expands to fill the time (Parkinson's Law in reverse), or you "vacation" for 30 days and return with no income generated.

For the typical user: **3-4 mornings a week, 9am-1pm local time, work-only blocks. Everything else is for the actual mini-retirement.**

## Step 8: Re-entry plan

The book underemphasizes this. Most mini-retirees come back and feel a sharp comedown.

Pre-plan:

- The **first project** you'll work on when you return (write it down before leaving)
- One thing from the mini-retirement that becomes a **permanent change at home** (a morning ritual, a removed habit, a new relationship, a creative practice)
- A **30-day decompression budget** — you will be less productive than usual for ~30 days; plan for it
- A **dreamline re-run** scheduled for 30-45 days post-return — the mini-retirement always changes the dreamline

## Common Failure Modes

- **Picking the destination first, lifestyle second.** Reverse it.
- **Underestimating wind-down.** It takes more time than you think.
- **No work split.** Either drift into vacation, or drift into accidentally-still-working.
- **Booking 3 months in one apartment sight unseen.** Book 2 weeks, then move.
- **Going back to exactly the same life.** The mini-retirement is wasted if no permanent change comes home with it.
- **No re-entry buffer.** Day 1 back, big project — predictable burnout.

## Special considerations

- **Partner & kids**: Whole different planning surface. Schools, custody, partner work — multiply the prep time. The book lightly addresses; many users will need to do additional research.
- **Health**: For chronic conditions, do not assume your meds and care will be available abroad. Pre-arrange with your home pharmacy / doctor.
- **Pets**: Either find a long-term sitter or accept that you can't (yet).
