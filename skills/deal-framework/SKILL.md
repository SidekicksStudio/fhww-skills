# The DEAL Framework — Full Walkthrough

You are running the user through the full **D-E-A-L** sequence — *The 4-Hour Workweek*'s end-to-end system, in order. Most people read the book and try to skip to L (Liberation) — work remote, take a sabbatical, move abroad — without first doing D (Definition: knowing what they want), E (Elimination: clearing the noise), and A (Automation: making the income source self-running).

Your job is to keep the order. The order is the value.

## Before Starting

Read `lifestyle-design.md` and `dreamline.md` if they exist. If neither does, run `/lifestyle-design` first or note that you'll capture the foundation lightly inside D.

Tell the user what's about to happen, in plain language:

> "We're going to walk through DEAL — Definition, Elimination, Automation, Liberation. In that order. Each phase has its own skill. I'll route you to the right one at each step. The total time, if you do it well, is roughly 4-8 weeks of work spread across 3-6 months of calendar — not one weekend. Want to start now or pick up where you left off?"

If they want to pick up partway through, jump to that phase using the routing table at the bottom.

## D — Definition

**Goal of this phase**: Know exactly what you want and what it costs.

**Mistake people make here**: Stay vague. "More freedom." "A better life." "Less stress." Then they skip to E.

**The two questions answered by the end of D**:

1. What does my new life actually look like, in dreams that are concrete and priced?
2. What is my **TDI** (Target Daily Income)?

### Run these skills, in order:

1. **`/lifestyle-design`** — capture current state, constraints, what you're optimizing for
2. **`/dreamlining`** — the 6-month / 12-month dreamline + TMI/TDI calculation
3. **`/fear-setting`** — run on the scariest action your dreamline implies
4. **`/relative-income`** — reframe income as $/hour × freedom (do this *especially* if your TDI implies "I need a higher salary"; often it doesn't)
5. **`/new-rich-rules`** — read once, helps when you stall

**Exit criterion before moving to E**:

You can answer in one breath:

> "By [date], I want to be doing [X], having [Y], and being [Z]. To fund this, I need $[TDI]/day. The action I've been most afraid of is [W]."

## E — Elimination

**Goal of this phase**: Free up the time you'll need to do anything in A and L. Stop doing the things that produce ≤ 20% of value.

**Mistake people make here**: Try to "do less" without identifying *which less*. They cut at random and slip back to baseline in 2 weeks.

**The two questions answered by the end of E**:

1. Of everything I do, which 20% drives ≥ 80% of results — and which 20% drives ≥ 80% of pain?
2. What is my **not-to-do list** — the recurring things I have explicitly stopped doing?

### Run these skills, in order:

1. **`/eighty-twenty`** — Pareto audit of customers, projects, time, stress
2. **`/parkinsons-law`** — shrink deadlines on what's left
3. **`/low-info-diet`** — stop consuming what you can't act on
4. **`/not-to-do-list`** — turn the audit into a list of explicit "stops"
5. **`/batching`** — group what's left into batches
6. **`/interruption-killer`** — kill the three types of interruption with scripts
7. **`/email-autoresponder`** — install Tim's email policy (often the single biggest win)

**Exit criterion before moving to A**:

You have removed at least **15 hours/week** from your schedule (or kept the hours but moved them to dream-aligned work). You have a written not-to-do list. Email is no longer something you check first thing.

## A — Automation

**Goal of this phase**: Replace yourself. Either with people, or with a product/system that runs without you.

**Mistake people make here**: Confuse "automation" with "tooling." Automation here means *removing yourself as the necessary input* — through delegation, productization, or both.

**The two questions answered by the end of A**:

1. Which decisions, tasks, and communications can be handled by someone (or something) other than me?
2. Is my income source — current job, current business, or new muse — set up to run without me?

### Run these skills, in order:

1. **`/outsourcing`** — virtual assistants and life delegation
2. **`/muse`** — evaluate or shape an automated income source (if your dreamline TDI requires one)
3. **`/muse-test`** — cheaply validate the muse before building
4. **`/income-autopilot`** — architect the management black box once a muse is real
5. (Optionally) loop **`/expert-research`** + **`/cold-outreach`** while waiting for tests to run, to talk to people who already automated similar work

**Exit criterion before moving to L**:

You can take a 4-week absence — **completely off** — and come back to a working income source. If you can't, A isn't done.

## L — Liberation

**Goal of this phase**: Use the freedom you just bought.

**Mistake people make here**: Skip directly to L without doing the prior three. They take a sabbatical funded by savings, run out, come back to a worse version of the same job.

**The two questions answered by the end of L**:

1. Where am I going to be, and why?
2. What am I going to do with the time that I couldn't do before?

### Run these skills, in order:

1. **`/remote-work-proposal`** — if you have a job and want to keep it but unanchor from a city
2. **`/mini-retirement`** — design a 1-6 month somewhere-else trip
3. **`/comfort-challenge`** — most people, when they have free time, freeze. The comfort challenges are the muscle for using freedom.

**Exit criterion**:

You are physically somewhere new for at least 30 days, with income flowing in without your daily input, working ≤ 20 hours per week, and the world has not collapsed. You then re-enter a Definition phase: now that you've done it, what do you actually want next?

(Because the answer "the same thing forever" is rare. New dreamlines are normal.)

## Routing Table

If the user is partway through, use this:

| User says | Run |
|---|---|
| "I haven't really started" | `/lifestyle-design` then `/dreamlining` |
| "I have a dreamline but I'm scared" | `/fear-setting` |
| "I'm too busy to do any of this" | `/eighty-twenty` then `/email-autoresponder` |
| "My job is fine but I'm chained to it" | `/eighty-twenty` then `/remote-work-proposal` |
| "I have a side project I want to turn into a muse" | `/muse` then `/muse-test` |
| "I'm running a muse and want to step away" | `/income-autopilot` then `/outsourcing` |
| "I have time and money and don't know what to do with it" | `/comfort-challenge` then re-run `/dreamlining` |
| "I want to live abroad for a few months" | `/mini-retirement` |

## Pace

Tim's argument is that you can do D in a weekend, E in 4 weeks, A in 1-3 months, L in whenever you book the flight. In practice for most users:

- D — 1 week of evenings, then re-do quarterly
- E — 4 weeks
- A — 8-12 weeks
- L — depends on visa / family / season

If a user is "doing all four at once in a single weekend retreat" — don't argue, but warn that the order exists for a reason and they'll likely circle back.

## What to Avoid

- Letting the user pick the phase they like best and skip the rest.
- Letting them mistake "thinking about each phase" for "doing each phase."
- Spending too long in any single skill. If a user has been in D for 6 weeks, push them into E with what they have.
- Treating this as one-and-done. The cycle is intended to repeat. New dreams trigger new DEAL passes.
